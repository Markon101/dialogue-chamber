The present paper has two aims. The first is the Brauer–Siegel theorem. For a number field
K with class number hK , regulator RK , and absolute discriminant dK , the theorem asserts
that log(hK RK ) ∼ 12 log dK as K runs over any sequence of Galois extensions of Q for which
[K : Q]/ log dK → 0, and its constants have long been ineffective because the proof passes
through Siegel’s theorem on L(1, χ). Stark [18] located the ineffectivity. If K/k is a normal
extension and ζK (s) has a real zero β close to one, then β is a zero of ζF (s) for some field F
with k ⊆ F ⊆ K and [F : k] ⩽ 2. For a field built by a tower of normal steps over Q, an almost
normal field in the terminology of later authors, the reduction continues down the tower, and
the only sources of ineffectivity are the quadratic subfields; Stark’s effective cases are the almost
normal fields without them. Now that Landau–Siegel zeros of quadratic characters over Q are
excluded [7,9,15] with absolute constants, the theorem is effective for every almost normal field,
in particular for every Galois extension of Q. That much is immediate from Stark’s reduction,
and we state it only in passing (Remark 3.4). Theorem 1.3 concerns the Galois extensions K of
an arbitrary number field k. Such a K need not be almost normal, and Stark’s reduction stops
at k, where two obstructions remain, the quadratic extensions F/k and the zeta function of k
itself. The first is removed by Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2, the exclusion of Landau–Siegel
zeros for the quadratic characters of k, and the second is a constant depending only on k. The
outcome is the Brauer–Siegel theorem, effective and uniform over all Galois extensions of a
fixed base field.
The second aim is the method. The argument of [8], carried out in a Galois extension K/k
of number fields with group G, with one variable for each element of a subset S ⊂ G containing
the identity, bounds the logarithmic mass of the primes of k whose Frobenius lies in S by the
Chebotarev density |S|/|G| of those primes, uniformly in K (Theorem 1.4). The constant is the
number of variables |S| divided by the rank [K : Q] of the lattice of points, times [k : Q]. In the
quadratic case K = F (ξ), with ξ a primitive ℓ-th root of unity and S the identity together with
the coset of Gal(K/F ) occupied by the inert primes, this is 12 + 1/[K : k], which can be made
as close to 21 as we please by letting ℓ → ∞. The only input from the field itself is Masser’s
√ √
invariant of the lattice, and its computation, a Galois-theoretic argument specific to Q( d, 2 )

Remark 7.1. The bound of Theorem 1.4 is one-sided, and the identity decides the side. The
hypothesis 1 ∈ S is a normalization, since the argument with ι0 (θ) as the distinguished variable,

determinant method, as used for example in [7, 9], imposes vanishing to high order in one
variable at the pairs (α, σα), with α running over about 2X small integers of K; the derivatives
supply the multiplicity that our box of N nK points supplies, the second variable carries degree
